\section{The G$_0$W self-energy}
\label{sec:selfenergy}
% ======================================================================

This section derives the self-energy that the package evaluates, \cref{eq:sigma} below, starting from the
Coulomb Hamiltonian of the stack. The derivation is standard many-body perturbation
theory~\cite{Hedin1965,HedinLundqvist1970,Mahan,BruusFlensberg,AryasetiawanGunnarsson1998}. What is
specific to a van der Waals stack is that the interaction is a matrix in the sheet index, and that the
electrons of a multilayer sample (the two layers of TBG) are coherent superpositions of states living on
different sheets. These two facts are why the layer-resolved density matrix elements appear in every
vertex. \Cref{fig:sigma_roadmap} shows the sequence of steps and where each one is done.

\subsection{Interaction Hamiltonian of the stack}
\label{sec:hint}

Let $\psi_{a\sigma}(\br)$ be the field operator of an electron on sheet $a$, sublattice $\sigma$, at in-plane
position $\br$, and $n_a(\br)=\sum_\sigma\psi^\dagger_{a\sigma}(\br)\psi_{a\sigma}(\br)$ the number density of
sheet $a$. Spin and valley indices are suppressed: the interaction is diagonal in both
(\cref{sec:degeneracy}), and the density operators below include the sum over them. Because the sheets are
infinitely thin (\cref{as:electro}), the charge density of the stack is $-e\sum_an_a(\br)\delta(z-z_a)$, and
the electrostatic energy is
\begin{equation}
H_{\rm int}=\frac12\sum_{ab}\int\dd^2r\,\dd^2r'\;n_a(\br)\,U_{ab}(\br-\br')\,n_b(\br') ,
\label{eq:Hint_real}
\end{equation}
where $U_{ab}(\br-\br')$ is the energy of an electron at $(\br,z_a)$ in the potential of a second electron at
$(\br',z_b)$, including all the image charges induced in the dielectrics and in the gates. It depends only
on $\br-\br'$ because the stack is homogeneous in the plane, and its Fourier transform is precisely the
kernel of \cref{sec:electrostatics}, $U_{ab}(\br)=A^{-1}\sum_{\bq}V_{ab}(q)e^{\ii\bq\cdot\br}$, where $A$ is the
normalisation area of the sample. With $n_a(\bq)=\int\dd^2r\,e^{-\ii\bq\cdot\br}n_a(\br)$,
\begin{equation}
H_{\rm int}=\frac1{2A}\sum_{\bq\neq0}\sum_{ab}V_{ab}(q)\,:\!n_a(\bq)\,n_b(-\bq)\!: .
\label{eq:Hint}
\end{equation}
The colons denote normal ordering, which removes the interaction of each electron with itself, and the
$\bq=0$ term is cancelled by the neutralising background of the gates and dopants. The density operator
obeys $n_a(\bq)^\dagger=n_a(-\bq)$.

\Cref{eq:Hint} is the reason for the layer index. $V_{ab}(q)$ is a genuine matrix: two electrons on the same
sheet interact through $V_{aa}$, two electrons on sheets a distance $d$ apart through
$V_{ab}\simeq V_{aa}e^{-\kappa d}$ (\cref{eq:bilayerV}), and a gate or a dielectric interface modifies the
two differently. The interaction energy of a density fluctuation therefore depends on how its charge is
distributed among the sheets, and the operator that couples to the Coulomb field of sheet $a$ is the
density of that sheet, $n_a(\bq)$, not the total density. In a monolayer sample this is trivial, since only one
sheet is involved, but in TBG a Bloch electron has amplitude on both layers and a transition between two
Bloch states moves charge on both, with different weights. These weights are the form factors.

\subsection{Layer-resolved density operator in the band basis}
\label{sec:formfactors}

In the continuum model of \cref{sec:tbgmodel} the field operator is expanded in plane waves of the moir\'e
reciprocal lattice,
\begin{equation}
\psi_{a\sigma}(\br)=\frac1{\sqrt A}\sum_{\bk\in{\rm BZ}}\sum_{\bG}e^{\ii(\bk+\bG)\cdot\br}\,c_{\bk+\bG,a\sigma},
\qquad
c_{\bk+\bG,a\sigma}=\sum_n u_{n\bk}(\bG,a,\sigma)\,c_{n\bk},
\label{eq:planewave}
\end{equation}
where $c_{n\bk}$ annihilates an electron in the Bloch eigenstate $n$ of $H(\bk)$ and $u_{n\bk}(\bG,a,\sigma)$ is the
component of that eigenvector on plane wave $\bG$, sheet $a$, sublattice $\sigma$ (stored as
\code{u[4 i\_G + 2a + sigma]}, $i_{\bG}$ the index of $\bG$). The momenta of both layers are measured from the same
origin $\bm\Gamma^{\rm lab}$ (\cref{eq:labframe}), so this common offset cancels in $\psi^\dagger\psi$. Inserting
\cref{eq:planewave} into the definition of $n_a(\bq)$ and using $\int\dd^2r\,e^{\ii(\bp'-\bp)\cdot\br}=A\,\delta_{\bp\bp'}$:
\begin{equation}
n_a(\bq)=\sum_{\bk\in{\rm BZ}}\sum_{\bG\sigma}c^\dagger_{\bk+\bG,a\sigma}\,c_{\bk+\bq+\bG,a\sigma} .
\end{equation}
If $\bk+\bq$ lies outside the first zone we write $\bk+\bq=\bk'+\bG_0$ with $\bk'$ in the zone
(\cref{fig:quadrature}a), so that $c_{\bk+\bq+\bG,a\sigma}=c_{\bk'+(\bG+\bG_0),a\sigma}$. Expanding both operators in
Bloch states gives
\begin{equation}
n_a(\bq)=\sum_{\bk}\sum_{nm}\rho^a_{nm}(\bk,\bq)\,c^\dagger_{n\bk}\,c_{m,\bk+\bq},
\label{eq:nq_band}
\end{equation}
with the \emph{form factors}
\begin{equation}
\rho^a_{nm}(\bk,\bq)=\sum_{\bG\sigma}u^*_{n\bk}(\bG,a,\sigma)\,u_{m\bk'}(\bG+\bG_0,a,\sigma)
=\bra{u_{n\bk}}P_a\,\hat\tau_{\bG_0}\ket{u_{m,\bk'}} ,
\label{eq:formfactor}
\end{equation}
with $c_{m,\bk+\bq}\equiv c_{m\bk'}$. Here $P_a$ projects on the two sublattices of sheet $a$ and $\hat\tau_{\bG_0}$
shifts the plane-wave index by $\bG_0$ (\code{densityL[a, n, m, k, q, 0]} in \code{LayerHelpers}; the mesh index of
$\bk'$ is \code{k\_plus\_q\_ind[q, k]}). The form factor $\rho^a_{nm}(\bk,\bq)$ is the amplitude for the transition
$m,\bk+\bq\to n,\bk$ to be accompanied by a density change of wavevector $\bq$ on sheet $a$. Three properties are
used below. Since $n_a(\bq)^\dagger=n_a(-\bq)$,
\begin{equation}
n_a(-\bq)=\sum_{\bk}\sum_{nm}\bigl[\rho^a_{nm}(\bk,\bq)\bigr]^*\,c^\dagger_{m,\bk+\bq}\,c_{n\bk} .
\label{eq:nminusq}
\end{equation}
Summing over the sheets of the sample gives the total-density matrix element, which at $\bq=0$ is the overlap of
two eigenvectors of the same Hamiltonian, $\sum_a\rho^a_{nm}(\bk,0)=\delta_{nm}$
(\code{test\_density\_matrix\_completeness}). Finally, sample Bloch states have no amplitude on the tip, because
tip and sample are not tunnel coupled (\cref{as:decoupled}): $\rho^{\rm tip}_{nm}=0$ for sample states, so only
the sample sheets appear in the vertices of the sample self-energy, while the tip enters through the screening.
For a momentum transfer $\bq+\bG$ that differs from $\bq$ by a moir\'e vector, the same construction gives the
umklapp form factors $\rho^a_{nm}(\bk,\bq;\bG)$ (last index of \code{densityL}). Only $\bG=0$ is kept in the
self-energy (\cref{as:head}). Physically this restricts the momentum transferred to the electron to the first
moir\'e zone.

For graphene there is one sheet, the plane-wave sum reduces to a single term, and the form factor is the
spinor overlap $\rho_{ss'}(\bk,\bq)=\braket{u^s_{\bk}}{u^{s'}_{\bk+\bq}}$, whose square is the chirality factor
of \cref{eq:chirality}.

\subsection{The exchange term: origin of the vertex structure}
\label{sec:fock}

The structure of the self-energy is already visible at first order in $V$. Insert \cref{eq:formfactor,eq:nminusq}
in \cref{eq:Hint}:
\begin{equation}
H_{\rm int}=\frac1{2A}\sum_{\bq}\sum_{ab}V_{ab}(q)\sum_{\bk\bk'}\sum_{nm n'm'}
\rho^a_{nm}(\bk,\bq)\bigl[\rho^b_{n'm'}(\bk',\bq)\bigr]^*\;
c^\dagger_{n\bk}\,c^\dagger_{m',\bk'+\bq}\,c_{n'\bk'}\,c_{m,\bk+\bq},
\end{equation}
where normal ordering has moved $c^\dagger_{m',\bk'+\bq}$ to the left at the price of a sign, compensated by
reversing the order of the two annihilation operators. The first-order self-energy follows from Wick's
theorem, i.e.\ from the mean-field decoupling of the four-operator product in the non-interacting ground
state, where $\langle c^\dagger_{m'\bp'}c_{m\bp}\rangle=\delta_{mm'}\delta_{\bp\bp'}\nF(\xi_{m\bp})$. The
\emph{exchange} contraction pairs $c^\dagger_{m',\bk'+\bq}$ with $c_{m,\bk+\bq}$. It requires $\bk'=\bk$ and
$m'=m$, and moving $c^\dagger_{m',\bk'+\bq}$ next to $c_{m,\bk+\bq}$ costs one more sign. The other exchange
contraction, of $c^\dagger_{n\bk}$ with $c_{n'\bk'}$, gives the same operator after relabelling $\bq\to-\bq$,
$a\leftrightarrow b$, and doubles the result, which cancels the factor $\frac12$. The two \emph{Hartree} contractions,
inside each density, set $\bq=0$, which is excluded. The result is a one-body operator
$\sum_{\bk}\sum_{nn'}\Sigma^x_{nn'}(\bk)\,c^\dagger_{n\bk}c_{n'\bk}$ with
\begin{equation}
\Sigma^x_{nn'}(\bk)=-\frac1A\sum_{\bq}\sum_m\sum_{ab}\rho^a_{nm}(\bk,\bq)\,V_{ab}(q)\,
\bigl[\rho^b_{n'm}(\bk,\bq)\bigr]^*\,\nF(\xi_{m,\bk+\bq}) .
\label{eq:fock}
\end{equation}
This is the Fock self-energy. It contains the three ingredients of the G$_0$W formula: the vertex
$\rho^a_{nm}$ that takes $n,\bk$ to $m,\bk+\bq$ while creating a density fluctuation on sheet $a$, the
interaction $V_{ab}$ that carries this fluctuation to sheet $b$, and the conjugate vertex
$[\rho^b_{n'm}]^*$ that brings the electron back to $n',\bk$. The overall minus sign is the fermionic exchange
sign, and the occupation $\nF(\xi_m)$ states that only occupied intermediate states contribute to exchange.
When $n\neq n'$ the result is a matrix in band space, because the electron can leave from band $n'$ and
return to band $n$ through the same intermediate state.

\subsection{Dynamical screening and the G$_0$W approximation}
\label{sec:gw}
\label{sec:sigma_derivation}

The exchange term uses the bare, instantaneous interaction. In the stack, the density fluctuation created at
the first vertex polarises all the other electrons, of the sample and of the tip, and the field that reaches
the second vertex is screened and retarded. Hedin's equations~\cite{Hedin1965} express the exact self-energy
as $\Sigma=\ii GW\Gamma$, with $W=V+VPW$ the screened interaction, $P$ the irreducible polarisability and
$\Gamma$ the vertex function. The GW approximation sets $\Gamma=1$, so that $P=-\ii GG$ is the RPA bubble and
$\Sigma=\ii GW$ is the Fock diagram with $V$ replaced by $W$
(\cref{fig:diagrams}b)~\cite{Hedin1965,HybertsenLouie1986,AryasetiawanGunnarsson1998,Golze2019}. In the one-shot
G$_0$W variant used here, $G$ is the non-interacting propagator of the sample and $W$ is built from the
non-interacting polarisability $\chi^0$ (\cref{as:gw}).

\paragraph{Screened interaction in sheet space.} The layer-resolved $W$ is the bare interaction dressed by the
density response of the stack,
\begin{gather}
W_{ab}(\bq,\ii\Omega_m)=V_{ab}(q)+\sum_{cd}V_{ac}(q)\,\chi_{cd}(\bq,\ii\Omega_m)\,V_{db}(q),
\label{eq:Wdef}\\
\chi_{cd}(\bq,\ii\Omega_m)=-\frac1A\int_0^\beta\!\dd\tau\,e^{\ii\Omega_m\tau}\bigl\langle T_\tau\,n_c(\bq,\tau)\,n_d(-\bq,0)\bigr\rangle ,
\nonumber
\end{gather}
where $\chi_{cd}$ is the response of the density of sheet $c$ to a potential applied to sheet $d$, $\beta=1/k_BT$
and $T_\tau$ orders in imaginary time. It is the effective interaction between a density fluctuation of
wavevector $\bq$ on sheet $a$ and one on sheet $b$, mediated by every polarisable sheet of the junction. In the
RPA $\chi=\chi^0+\chi^0V\chi$, which gives $W=V+V\chi^0W$ (\cref{fig:diagrams}c) and therefore the matrix
relation $W=\varepsilon^{-1}V$ of \cref{eq:epsW}. The Lindhard function $\chi^0_{cd}$ of \cref{eq:chi0} is built
with the same form factors, two per bubble, one for each sheet index.

\paragraph{Matsubara form.} The Feynman rules of the imaginary-time
formalism~\cite{Mahan,BruusFlensberg,FetterWalecka} assign to diagram~\ref{fig:diagrams}b a propagator
$G^0_m(\bk+\bq,\ii\omega_n+\ii\Omega_m)$ for the internal line, the interaction $W_{ab}(\bq,\ii\Omega_m)$, a vertex
factor at each end, a factor $1/(\beta A)$ for the free sums over $\bq$ and over the bosonic frequency, and the
exchange sign. The result is
\begin{equation}
\Sigma_{nn'}(\bk,\ii\omega_n)=-\frac1{\beta A}\sum_{\bq}\sum_{\ii\Omega_m}\sum_m\sum_{ab}
\rho^a_{nm}(\bk,\bq)\,W_{ab}(\bq,\ii\Omega_m)\,\bigl[\rho^b_{n'm}(\bk,\bq)\bigr]^*\,
G^0_m(\bk+\bq,\ii\omega_n+\ii\Omega_m),
\label{eq:sigma_matsubara}
\end{equation}
with the fermionic and bosonic Matsubara frequencies $\omega_n=(2n+1)\pi/\beta$ and $\Omega_m=2m\pi/\beta$, and the
non-interacting propagator $G^0_m(\bp,\ii\omega_n)=1/(\ii\omega_n-\xi_{m\bp})$. Energy is conserved at the vertices,
which is why the bosonic frequency $\Omega_m$ carried by $W$ appears in the internal electron line. The
indices $a,b$ run over the sheets of the sample, $m$ over the bands included in the calculation, and
$\frac1A\sum_{\bq}\to\int\dd^2q/(2\pi)^2$ in the thermodynamic limit.

The sign and the prefactor of \cref{eq:sigma_matsubara} can be checked against \cref{eq:fock}. For a static
interaction, $W\to V$, the frequency sum involves only the propagator. With the convergence factor
$e^{\ii\Omega_m0^+}$ that accompanies an equal-time interaction~\cite{Mahan},
\begin{equation}
\frac1\beta\sum_{\ii\Omega_m}\frac{e^{\ii\Omega_m0^+}}{\ii\Omega_m-(\xi_m-\ii\omega_n)}=-\nB(\xi_m-\ii\omega_n)=\nF(\xi_m),
\end{equation}
where the last step uses $e^{-\ii\beta\omega_n}=-1$. \Cref{eq:sigma_matsubara} then reduces to \cref{eq:fock}, as it
must.

\subsection{Spectral representation of $W$ and the frequency sum}

Split the interaction into its bare and dynamical parts, $W=V+W_c$. The bare part gives the exchange term
\eqref{eq:fock}, which is not included (\cref{sec:exchange}). The dynamical part is analytic in the complex
frequency plane away from the real axis and vanishes for $|z|\to\infty$, because the polarisability does. For a finite set of bands $\chi^0(\bq,z)\propto z^{-2}$
(the $z^{-1}$ term is the expectation value of a commutator of densities, which vanishes, and the $z^{-2}$ term is fixed
by the f-sum rule~\cite{GiulianiVignale}), while for the unbounded Dirac cone $\chi^0\propto z^{-1}$. It therefore obeys the spectral representation
\eqref{eq:spectralrep},
\begin{equation}
W_c(\bq,z)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\dd\nu\,\frac{S(\bq,\nu)}{z-\nu},\qquad
S(\bq,\nu)=\frac{\ii}{2\pi}\bigl[\WR(\bq,\nu)-\WR(\bq,\nu)^\dagger\bigr],
\label{eq:Wc_spectral}
\end{equation}
which gives the retarded function for $\Imm z>0$, the advanced one for $\Imm z<0$ (using
$W^{\rm A}=(\WR)^\dagger$), and the Matsubara function at $z=\ii\Omega_m$. Inserted in
\cref{eq:sigma_matsubara}, the only dependence on $\ii\Omega_m$ is in the product of two simple poles, and the
frequency sum is
\begin{equation}
\frac1\beta\sum_{\ii\Omega_m}\frac{1}{(\ii\Omega_m-\nu)(\ii\Omega_m+\ii\omega_n-\xi_m)}
=-\frac{\nB(\nu)+\nF(\xi_m)}{\ii\omega_n+\nu-\xi_m} .
\label{eq:bosesum}
\end{equation}

The derivation is the standard contour argument~\cite{Mahan,BruusFlensberg} (\cref{fig:contour}). Write
$F(z)=1/[(z-\nu)(z+\ii\omega_n-\xi_m)]$. The function $\nB(z)=1/(e^{\beta z}-1)$ has simple poles with residue
$1/\beta$ at $z=\ii\Omega_m$, hence $\frac1\beta\sum_mF(\ii\Omega_m)=\oint_C\frac{\dd z}{2\pi\ii}\nB(z)F(z)$ along a contour
$C$ that encloses the imaginary axis counter-clockwise. Since $F\sim z^{-2}$ the contour can be opened onto a circle at
infinity, which contributes nothing, and what remains are the two poles of $F$, encircled clockwise:
\begin{equation}
\frac1\beta\sum_{\ii\Omega_m}F(\ii\Omega_m)=-\frac{\nB(\nu)}{\nu+\ii\omega_n-\xi_m}-\frac{\nB(\xi_m-\ii\omega_n)}{\xi_m-\ii\omega_n-\nu}.
\end{equation}
Because $\omega_n$ is a fermionic frequency, $\nB(\xi_m-\ii\omega_n)=1/(-e^{\beta\xi_m}-1)=-\nF(\xi_m)$, and the two terms
combine into \cref{eq:bosesum}. Using it in \cref{eq:sigma_matsubara} with $W\to W_c$, the two minus signs cancel:
\begin{equation}
\Sigma^c_{nn'}(\bk,\ii\omega_n)=\frac1A\sum_{\bq}\sum_m\sum_{ab}\rho^a_{nm}(\bk,\bq)\bigl[\rho^b_{n'm}(\bk,\bq)\bigr]^*
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\dd\nu\;S_{ab}(\bq,\nu)\,\frac{\nB(\nu)+\nF(\xi_m)}{\ii\omega_n+\nu-\xi_m},
\end{equation}
with the shorthand $\xi_m\equiv\xi_{m,\bk+\bq}$ for the energy of the intermediate state.

% ----------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Analytic continuation to real frequency}

The Matsubara self-energy now depends on $\ii\omega_n$ only through the denominator, since the factor
$e^{-\ii\beta\omega_n}=-1$ has already been used. It is the value at $z=\ii\omega_n$ of a function analytic off the real axis
and decaying at infinity, and this continuation is unique~\cite{BaymMermin1961}. The retarded self-energy, which is the
one that enters the Dyson equation for the spectral function, is obtained with $\ii\omega_n\to\eps+\ii0^+$:
\begin{equation}
\Sigma^{c}_{nn'}(\bk,\eps)=\int\!\frac{\dd^2q}{(2\pi)^2}\sum_m\sum_{ab}\rho^a_{nm}(\bk,\bq)\,\bigl[\rho^b_{n'm}(\bk,\bq)\bigr]^*
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\dd\nu\;S_{ab}(\bq,\nu)\,\frac{\nB(\nu)+\nF(\xi_m)}{\eps+\nu-\xi_m+\ii0^+} .
\label{eq:sigma_full_line}
\end{equation}
This is Eqs.~(37) and (39) of Ref.~\cite{Wei2025}, with the sheet index in place of the moir\'e $\bG$ index. In
\cref{eq:sigma_full_line} $\nu$ runs over the whole real axis, and the spectral density at negative $\nu$ is not stored
by the code. The next step shows that it is not needed.

% ----------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Folding onto positive frequencies}

\paragraph{Symmetry of $S$.} Two properties of the response of the stack fix $S$ at $\nu<0$. First, the density
$n_a(\br)$ is a Hermitian operator, so the retarded response to a real potential is real in real space and time.
In Fourier space this reads $\WR_{ab}(\bq,-\nu)=[\WR_{ab}(-\bq,\nu)]^*$, and inserting it in \cref{eq:Wc_spectral},
\begin{equation}
S(\bq,-\nu)=\frac{\ii}{2\pi}\Bigl[\WR(-\bq,\nu)^*-\WR(-\bq,\nu)^T\Bigr]
=-\Bigl\{\frac{\ii}{2\pi}\bigl[\WR(-\bq,\nu)-\WR(-\bq,\nu)^\dagger\bigr]\Bigr\}^T=-S(-\bq,\nu)^T .
\end{equation}
Second, for a time-reversal-symmetric sample the Onsager relation $\chi^0(-\bq,\nu)=\chi^0(\bq,\nu)^T$
holds~\cite{GiulianiVignale} (for TBG it requires both valleys, \cref{eq:valleysym}). Since $V$ is symmetric,
$W(-\bq,\nu)=W(\bq,\nu)^T$ and $S(-\bq,\nu)^T=S(\bq,\nu)$. Together,
\begin{equation}
S(\bq,-\nu)=-S(\bq,\nu):
\label{eq:Sodd}
\end{equation}
the spectral density is odd in frequency, positive semidefinite at $\nu>0$ (absorption by the stack) and negative
semidefinite at $\nu<0$.

\paragraph{Folding.} Split the $\nu$ integral of \cref{eq:sigma_full_line} at $\nu=0$ and substitute $\nu=-\omega$ in the negative
half. With \cref{eq:Sodd}, $\nB(-\omega)=-1-\nB(\omega)$ and $1-\nF(\xi)=\nF(-\xi)$,
\begin{align}
\int_{-\infty}^0\!\dd\nu\,S(\bq,\nu)\frac{\nB(\nu)+\nF(\xi_m)}{\eps+\nu-\xi_m+\ii0^+}
&=\int_0^\infty\!\dd\omega\,\bigl[-S(\bq,\omega)\bigr]\frac{-1-\nB(\omega)+\nF(\xi_m)}{\eps-\omega-\xi_m+\ii0^+}\nonumber\\
&=\int_0^\infty\!\dd\omega\,S(\bq,\omega)\frac{\nB(\omega)+\nF(-\xi_m)}{\eps-\omega-\xi_m+\ii0^+} .
\end{align}
Adding the positive half, which is unchanged,
\begin{multline}
\Sigma^c_{nn'}(\bk,\eps)=\int\!\frac{\dd^2q}{(2\pi)^2}\sum_m\sum_{ab}\rho^a_{nm}\bigl[\rho^b_{n'm}\bigr]^*\\
\times\int_0^\infty\!\dd\omega\;S_{ab}(\bq,\omega)\left[\frac{\nB(\omega)+\nF(-\xi_m)}{\eps-\xi_m-\omega+\ii0^+}
+\frac{\nB(\omega)+\nF(\xi_m)}{\eps-\xi_m+\omega+\ii0^+}\right].
\label{eq:sigma_folded}
\end{multline}
The code writes the bracket with the opposite sign of the denominators, which makes the sign of $\Imm\Sigma$ manifest.
Replacing the integral over $\bq$ by the quadrature of \cref{sec:radial}, the infinitesimal $0^+$ by a small but finite
$\eta_\Sigma$, and the upper limit by the largest frequency $\omega_{\max}$ of the grid on which $S$ is known,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\Sigma_{nn'}(\bk,\eps)=-\sum_{\bq}w_{\bq}\sum_m\sum_{ab}\rho^a_{nm}(\bk,\bq)\bigl[\rho^b_{n'm}(\bk,\bq)\bigr]^*
\int_0^{\omega_{\max}}\!\dd\omega\;S_{ab}(\bq,\omega)\,\mathcal D_m(\omega,\eps)}
\label{eq:sigma}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\mathcal D_m(\omega,\eps)=\frac{N^+_m(\omega)}{\omega+\xi_m-\eps-\ii\eta_\Sigma}+\frac{N^-_m(\omega)}{-\omega+\xi_m-\eps-\ii\eta_\Sigma},
\label{eq:D}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
N^\pm_m(\omega)=\nB(\omega)+\nF(\mp\xi_m)\ \xrightarrow{T=0}\ \Theta(\pm\xi_m).
\label{eq:Npm}
\end{equation}
Here $w_{\bq}$ is the weight of the momentum quadrature (\cref{sec:radial}), $\xi_m=\xi_{m,\bk+\bq}$ is the energy of the
intermediate state measured from the chemical potential, $N^\pm_m$ are its population factors, and the label $\Sigma$
stands for the correlation part $\Sigma^c$ from now on. \Cref{eq:sigma} is implemented in
\code{compute\_self\_energy\_band\_matrix} (TBG, band matrix, direct frequency contraction) and in its fast version
\code{compute\_self\_energy\_band\_matrix\_kernel} (\cref{sec:kernel}). The diagonal elements alone are computed by
\code{compute\_self\_energy\_full\_frequency\_pybandstructure}, which returns the summand of every pair $(a,b)$ separately
so that their sum is $\Sigma_{nn}$. For graphene the same formula is evaluated by \code{slg\_self\_energy.sigma\_full\_frequency\_fast}
and \code{sigma\_full\_frequency\_kernel} on the radial grid of \cref{sec:radial}.

% ----------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Physical content}
\label{sec:sigma_physics}

At $T=0$ the Bose factor vanishes for $\omega>0$ and the populations become step functions. \Cref{eq:sigma_folded} then
reads
\begin{equation}
\Sigma_{nn'}(\bk,\eps)=\int\!\frac{\dd^2q}{(2\pi)^2}\sum_m\sum_{ab}\rho^a_{nm}\bigl[\rho^b_{n'm}\bigr]^*\int_0^\infty\!\dd\omega\,
S_{ab}(\bq,\omega)\left[\frac{\Theta(\xi_m)}{\eps-\xi_m-\omega+\ii0^+}+\frac{\Theta(-\xi_m)}{\eps-\xi_m+\omega+\ii0^+}\right],
\label{eq:sigmaT0}
\end{equation}
the form familiar from GW calculations in solids~\cite{HybertsenLouie1986,AryasetiawanGunnarsson1998}. Its
anti-Hermitian part follows from $\Imm(x+\ii0^+)^{-1}=-\pi\delta(x)$. For the diagonal element,
\begin{equation}
-\Imm\Sigma_{nn}(\bk,\eps)=\pi\int\!\frac{\dd^2q}{(2\pi)^2}\sum_m\Bigl[\Theta(\xi_m)\,\Theta(\eps-\xi_m)\,\mathcal S_{nm}(\bq,\eps-\xi_m)
+\Theta(-\xi_m)\,\Theta(\xi_m-\eps)\,\mathcal S_{nm}(\bq,\xi_m-\eps)\Bigr],
\label{eq:ImSigmaT0}
\end{equation}
with $\mathcal S_{nm}(\bq,\omega)=\sum_{ab}\rho^a_{nm}S_{ab}(\bq,\omega)[\rho^b_{nm}]^*\ge0$, the spectral density of $W$ seen through
the vertex of the transition $n\to m$. This is Fermi's golden rule (\cref{fig:processes}). An electron added at energy
$\eps>0$ decays into an empty state $m,\bk+\bq$ of lower energy $\xi_m\in(0,\eps)$ by emitting a boson (a plasmon or a
particle--hole pair) of momentum $-\bq$ and energy $\omega=\eps-\xi_m$, and the rate is $2|\Imm\Sigma|$. A hole added at
$\eps<0$ decays in the mirror way into an occupied state with $\eps<\xi_m<0$. An electron at the Fermi level cannot
decay, $\Imm\Sigma(\eps=0)=0$, because the phase space of both processes closes. The real part of $\Sigma$ follows from
the imaginary part by a Kramers--Kronig relation and describes the energy shift of the quasiparticle by virtual
emission and reabsorption.

% ----------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Momentum and frequency quadrature}
\label{sec:radial}

\Cref{eq:sigma} is evaluated as a finite double sum,
\begin{equation}
\Sigma_{nn'}(\bk,\eps)\simeq-\sum_{\bq}w_{\bq}\sum_{m}\sum_{ab}\rho^a_{nm}(\bk,\bq)\bigl[\rho^b_{n'm}(\bk,\bq)\bigr]^*
\sum_{j=1}^{n_\omega}t_j\,S_{ab}(\bq,\omega_j)\,\mathcal D_m(\omega_j,\eps),
\label{eq:sigma_discrete}
\end{equation}
where $\omega_j$ are the points of the frequency grid and $t_j$ their trapezoidal weights,
$t_j=\frac12(\omega_{j+1}-\omega_{j-1})$ with half intervals at the two ends (\code{compat.trapezoid\_weights}). The momentum
weights $w_{\bq}$ (in nm$^{-2}$) are defined such that $\sum_{\bq}w_{\bq}f(\bq)$ approximates $\int\dd^2q\,f(\bq)/(2\pi)^2$. Their
construction depends on the sample (\cref{fig:quadrature}).

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{figures/fig_quadrature.png}
\caption{Momentum quadratures. (a) TBG: $\bk$ and $\bq$ run over the same $N_s\times N_s$ mesh of the moir\'e Brillouin
zone, every point with the same weight. When $\bk+\bq$ falls outside the zone it is folded back by a moir\'e vector
$\bG_0$, which the form factor \eqref{eq:formfactor} compensates by shifting the plane-wave index. (b) Graphene: the
spectral density depends only on $q=|\bq|$, so $\bq$ is sampled on rings of radius $q_i$ around the external momentum,
with $n_\vartheta$ directions per ring, and the intermediate states sit at $\bk-\bq$.}
\label{fig:quadrature}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Moir\'e mesh (TBG).} A finite periodic sample with $N_k$ moir\'e unit cells of area $A_{\rm cell}$ has
$A=N_kA_{\rm cell}$ and $N_k$ allowed momenta in the zone, and the area of the zone is
$A_{\rm BZ}=(2\pi)^2/A_{\rm cell}=\frac{\sqrt3}{2}|\bG_M|^2$. The exact sum of \cref{eq:sigma_matsubara} is therefore
\begin{equation}
\frac1A\sum_{\bq}f(\bq)=\frac{A_{\rm BZ}}{(2\pi)^2}\,\frac1{N_k}\sum_{\bq}f(\bq)\ \xrightarrow{N_k\to\infty}\ \int_{\rm BZ}\frac{\dd^2q}{(2\pi)^2}f(\bq),
\qquad
w_{\bq}=\frac{A_{\rm BZ}}{(2\pi)^2}\,\mathrm{weight}(\bq)
\label{eq:qweights}
\end{equation}
(\code{q\_weights\_from\_band\_structure}). Here $\mathrm{weight}(\bq)$ is the fraction of the zone attributed to the mesh
point by \texttt{pybandstructure} (\code{q\_sample.weights}, normalised to one). For the $N_s\times N_s$ Wigner--Seitz mesh
used here $N_k=N_s^2$ and every point has $\mathrm{weight}=1/N_s^2$, so $w_{\bq}=A_{\rm BZ}/(2\pi N_s)^2$ and
$\sum_{\bq}w_{\bq}=1/A_{\rm cell}$. The weight contains no spin or valley degeneracy (\cref{sec:degeneracy}). The external
momentum $\bk$ must itself be a mesh point, so that $\bk+\bq$ folds onto the mesh. The point $\bq=0$ is removed:
there $V$ diverges, but the singularity is integrable ($\int\dd^2q/q\propto\int\dd q$), and dropping the cell around
$\bq=0$ changes $\Sigma$ by an amount proportional to its linear size $|\bG_M|/N_s$, which vanishes as the mesh is refined.

\paragraph{Radial grid (isotropic samples).} For graphene $S(\bq,\omega)$ depends only on $|\bq|$, while the intermediate
energy $\xi^{s'}_{\bk-\bq}=s'\hbar v_F|\bk-\bq|-E_F$ and the chirality factor depend on the direction of $\bq$ relative to
$\bk$. The measure is split into a radial and an angular part,
\begin{equation}
\int\frac{\dd^2q}{(2\pi)^2}f(\bk,\bq)=\int_0^{q_{\max}}\frac{q\,\dd q}{2\pi}\,\bigl\langle f(\bk,\bq(\vartheta))\bigr\rangle_\vartheta,
\qquad\langle f\rangle_\vartheta=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\!\dd\vartheta\,f\simeq\frac1{n_\vartheta}\sum_{j=0}^{n_\vartheta-1}f(\vartheta_j),
\label{eq:radial}
\end{equation}
with $\vartheta_j=2\pi j/n_\vartheta$ (\code{make\_angular\_quadrature}) and the radial weights $w_{q_i}=q_i\,\Delta q_i/2\pi$,
$\Delta q_i=\frac12(q_{i+1}-q_{i-1})$ and half intervals at the ends (\code{make\_radial\_q\_weights}). The code places
the intermediate state at $\bk-\bq$ instead of $\bk+\bq$, which is equivalent because $\bq\to-\bq$ leaves both the measure
and $S(|\bq|,\omega)$ unchanged. The angular average cannot be skipped: sampling only $\vartheta=0$ (collinear $\bq$)
computes a different quantity, not a coarse version of the same one. Defaults: $q\in[0.02,3]k_F$ with $n_q=96$,
$n_\vartheta=64$, and $\omega\in(0,2.8E_F]$ with $n_\omega=480$.

% ----------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Causality}
\label{sec:passivity}

The anti-Hermitian part of \cref{eq:sigma} is, for $\eta_\Sigma\to0$,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\Sigma-\Sigma^\dagger}{2\ii}=-\pi\sum_{\bq}w_{\bq}\sum_m\Bigl[N^+_m\,\mathcal F_m\,S(\bq,\eps-\xi_m)\,\mathcal F_m^\dagger
+N^-_m\,\mathcal F_m\,S(\bq,\xi_m-\eps)\,\mathcal F_m^\dagger\Bigr],
\end{equation}
with the vertex matrix $(\mathcal F_m)_{na}=\rho^a_{nm}(\bk,\bq)$, the populations evaluated at the frequency where $S$ is
taken,
and $S(\bq,\omega)$ understood to vanish for $\omega<0$. It is a sum of negative semidefinite matrices when
$S\succeq0$ and $N^\pm\ge0$. Hence $\Imm\Sigma_{nn}\le0$ and the spectral function of \cref{sec:spectral} is positive. This
holds exactly with the Hermitian spectral density of \cref{eq:Wc_spectral} and is verified numerically
(\code{spectral.is\_passive}).

% ----------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Spin and valley degeneracy}
\label{sec:degeneracy}

The Coulomb potential varies on the scale of the interlayer distance, much longer than the lattice constant, so a
scattering event conserves spin and does not transfer an electron between valleys, which would require a momentum
$|\bK-\bK'|\sim1/a$ at which $V$ is negligible~\cite{Kotov2012}. The polarisation bubble \eqref{eq:chi0} is a closed fermion
loop and sums over the $g=4$ flavours of the particle--hole pair, so $g$ enters $\chi^0$, $\varepsilon$ and $W$. In
\cref{eq:sigma_matsubara}, instead, the internal electron line is the continuation of the external one and carries its
flavour. There is no free flavour sum and hence \emph{no} degeneracy factor in $w_{\bq}$. Including one would make
$\Sigma$ four times too large. Spin and valley enter the self-energy only through the screening.

% ----------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{What is not included: exchange and double counting}
\label{sec:exchange}

Only the correlation part is computed. The spectral density $S$ is that of $W$, and $V$ has no spectral weight for a
static background, so the exchange term \eqref{eq:fock} is absent. $\Sigma^x$ is real and static. It shifts and reshapes
the quasiparticle bands but cannot produce satellites or finite lifetimes. Ref.~\cite{Wei2025} (Sec.~IV\,B) likewise
neglects the first-order Hartree and Fock diagrams, with a normal-ordered Coulomb interaction. The consequences differ
between the two samples.

For TBG at neutrality the Hartree potential of the BM model vanishes, while the Fock term would renormalise the flat
bands. The correlation-only $\Sigma$ is therefore the self-energy of the scheme of Ref.~\cite{Wei2025}, and the
quasiparticle peaks are the bare continuum bands dressed by $\Ree\Sigma_c$ only, which should be kept in mind when
comparing with experiment.

For doped graphene the omitted $\Sigma^x$ is what enhances the Fermi velocity~\cite{Kotov2012,Elias2011}. Without it the
correlation part alone narrows the band ($v^*/v_F\simeq0.92$ in notebook 02). Since $\Sigma^x$ is real, it leaves
$\Imm\Sigma$, and hence the energy of the satellite structure, unchanged. It does move the quasiparticle peak by
$\Sigma^x_{nn}(\bk)$ and therefore changes the offset between peak and satellite. Quasiparticle velocities and
peak--satellite offsets of doped graphene computed here are correlation-only quantities. $\Sigma^x$ can be added to the
Dyson equation as a static term when needed.

% ----------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Plasmon-pole form}

Inserting $S(\bq,\omega)\to\sum_pR_p(\bq)\,\delta(\omega-\omega_p(\bq))$ (\cref{sec:ppa}) in \cref{eq:sigma} gives
\begin{equation}
\Sigma^{\rm PPA}_{nn'}(\bk,\eps)=-\sum_{\bq}w_{\bq}\sum_{m,p}\sum_{ab}\rho^a_{nm}\bigl[\rho^b_{n'm}\bigr]^*R_{p,ab}(\bq)\,
\mathcal D_m(\omega_p(\bq),\eps)
\end{equation}
(\code{compute\_self\_energy\_layer}, \code{compute\_self\_energy\_pybandstructure}), where $p$ labels the collective modes at
$\bq$, $\omega_p(\bq)$ their energies and $R_p(\bq)$ their residue matrices, \cref{eq:residue}. The full residue matrix is
used. A rank-one factorisation $R_p\simeq\Phi_p\Phi_p^\dagger$ is available as a diagnostic only. The plasmon-pole
approximation~\cite{HybertsenLouie1986,GodbyNeeds1989} ignores the particle--hole continuum, which carries most of the weight
of $S$ in the regimes studied here (\cref{sec:ppa}).

% ----------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Fast route: tabulated resolvent kernels}
\label{sec:kernel}

In \cref{eq:sigma,eq:D} the energies $\xi_m$ and $\eps$ appear in the denominators only through $u=\xi_m-\eps$, while the
populations split as $N^\pm_m=\nB(\omega)+\nF(\mp\xi_m)$. Therefore
\begin{equation}
\int_0^{\omega_{\max}}\!\dd\omega\,S(\bq,\omega)\mathcal D_m(\omega,\eps)=\nF(-\xi_m)\,P_{\bq}(u)+\nF(\xi_m)\,M_{\bq}(u)+B^+_{\bq}(u)+B^-_{\bq}(u),
\label{eq:kernel}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
P_{\bq}(u)=\int\dd\omega\frac{S(\bq,\omega)}{\omega+u-\ii\eta_\Sigma},\quad
M_{\bq}(u)=\int\dd\omega\frac{S(\bq,\omega)}{-\omega+u-\ii\eta_\Sigma},\quad
B^\pm_{\bq}(u)=\int\dd\omega\frac{\nB(\omega)S(\bq,\omega)}{\pm\omega+u-\ii\eta_\Sigma}.
\end{equation}
These four matrix-valued functions of a single variable depend on $\bq$ but not on $\bk$, $m$ or $\eps$. They are tabulated
once on a uniform $u$ grid of spacing $\eta_\Sigma/8$, with the same trapezoidal rule in $\omega$, and linearly interpolated
for every $(\bk,m,\eps)$ (\code{ResolventKernel}). This is an exact rewriting up to the interpolation error
($\lesssim2\times10^{-4}$ relative in the tests) and makes the cost per external $\bk$ independent of $n_\omega$. For graphene
it is about 300 times faster than the direct contraction, which makes full twist maps cheap. At $T=0$ the Bose terms
vanish because the $\omega$ grid starts above zero.
